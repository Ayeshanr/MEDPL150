Greater New York exhibition at PS1 MoMA

I visited the Greater New York 2026 exhibition at MoMA PS1. The "Greater New York 2026" exhibition brings together more than 50 artists of diverse cultures and age groups. The artworks range from many types of art such as photography, installations, papier-mache, and tin foil sculptures. In the exhibition, the central theme was the life of people living in New York in 2026. In particular, the exhibition showcased the contribution of people who help keep the city running, including cab drivers, delivery bikers, and the people. The exhibition shows the diversity, creativity, and fast pace of life in New York through many different artistic styles and materials.

One of the ideas that many of the artists in Greater New York 2026 explore is the idea that everyone and everything is connected. This idea was shown clearly in María-Elena Pombo’s installation, which was made from reddish yarn hanging down from the ceiling. The artist created the yarn using avocado pits and water collected from different places around the world, including Nigeria, Japan, Venezuela, and also some areas in the United States. By using materials connected to many different countries, Pombo shows that New York City is part of a much larger global system. The hanging yarn also looked fragile and uneven, which made the installation feel delicate and emotional. I interpreted this as a reflection on migration and the way people from many different cultures come together in New York City. Even though the threads looked separate at times, they were all connected together as part of one installation. This could symbolize how people in New York come from different backgrounds but still help shape the city together. The work also reflects the fast-moving and constantly changing nature of city life. Overall, I thought this installation was very powerful and interesting because it used simple materials to express ideas about identity, migration, globalization, and human connection.

Another artwork that I found very cool was Dean Millien’s The Cats and the Rats, which was made out of aluminum foil. At first, the piece looked playful and funny because of the different animal figures and the shiny material. However, the more I looked at it, the more I felt that the artwork had a deeper meaning. The cats appeared larger and more powerful, while the rats looked smaller and weaker. I interpreted this as representing power, survival, and the struggles that exist in everyday life, especially in a large city like New York. It also reminded me of Tom and Jerry, which made the piece feel more playful and familiar even though it still carried a deeper message underneath. Moreover, the aluminum foil material created a fragile image of the sculptures rather than making them seem strong and permanent. Although aluminum foil is a household product, the artist was able to transform it into lifelike animals, making the work innovative and fascinating. The bright silver coating surface gave the installation an artificial feeling, almost like the animals were part of a fantasy world instead of nature. I believe that through his work, the artist intends to communicate the chaos, competition, and unpredictability of city life. Overall, I liked this piece because it combined humor with deeper social ideas and turned ordinary materials into something expressive and memorable.

Generally, I have had a very fun experience at the exhibition since every painting depicted the modern world in its own way. While the artworks were created using distinct material and styles, most of them addressed such topics as identity, media culture, image, and city life in New York. Visiting the exhibition has been helpful in learning more about the techniques that modern artists use when depicting their vision of modern society.
