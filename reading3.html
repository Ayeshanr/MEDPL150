Motherhood in crisis by Laurence Ivil, Alicia Prager and Saidu Bah

In the graphic novel Motherhood in Crisis, Laurence Ivil, Alicia Prager, and Saidu Bah examine how systemic poverty and maternal healthcare determine survival. Although the narrative focuses on women in Sierra Leone, their stories feel universally urgent. The authors argue that high maternal mortality is not just a medical failure but a structural one influenced by geography and tradition. Reading this text has made me reconsider the privilege of medical access and the hidden dangers of giving birth in a broken system.

Motherhood in crisis by Laurence Ivil, Alicia Prager and Saidu Bah

In "Motherhood in Crisis" by Laurence Ivil, Alicia Prager, and Saidu Bah, the story highlights the danger of motherhood in Sierra Leone. Even though I was already aware that pregnancy was not easy and that there could be many complications, this reading made me realize that I had never really thought about pregnancy in other parts of the world. The fact that about one in ten women will lose her life during childbirth is shocking and difficult to understand. The novel shows us that even something as natural as giving birth can be very dangerous depending on where you live. In many ways, it helped me reflect on how differently people experience life from birth to death, and it changed the way I think about healthcare, equality, and survival.

The stories of Memunatu, Heela, Kadiatu, and Tetteh each show different struggles, but they all connect to the same issue of limited healthcare and inequality. In Memunatu's case, the problem of not being able to get medical assistance when living far from a hospital facility is demonstrated. She has to travel long distances while pregnant, which increases the risk of complications. This made me realize that while many people face healthcare issues, in her case it becomes a matter of life or death. Heela's story illustrates the problems associated with young pregnancies. As a young girl, she loses opportunities like education and independence. This ties into gender inequality since the man usually escapes all the consequences whereas the girl suffers the consequences. Kadiatu’s story brings up feelings of frustration for me since it describes the emotional trauma suffered by the mother and it shows us that these tragedies can be prevented if women had better medical assistance.Tetteh’s story is different because it shows how women can be treated unfairly by systems like the correctional system. Instead of receiving support, she is punished, which shows that the issue is not only medical but also social.

The graphic novel format makes these stories more powerful because the images bring the experiences to life. Seeing the emotions on the women’s faces made it easier to connect with their struggles compared to just reading facts. Through visuals, it is possible to convey such emotions effectively as well as the strength and resilience the women demonstrated during the difficult experiences they underwent. Moreover, the book highlighted how the outbreak of a virus such as COVID-19 has intensified these difficulties even further.

Ultimately, “Motherhood in Crisis” changed the way I think about pregnancy and motherhood. I now understand that for many women it is a dangerous experience, not just a normal and safe part of life. The stories made me think about the importance of improving healthcare systems and addressing gender inequality. I think that moving forward it is important for more people to learn about these realities. Knowing these differences can help create awareness and push for change.
