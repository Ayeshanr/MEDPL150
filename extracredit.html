Greater New York exhibition at PS1 MoMA

I visited the Greater New York 2026 exhibition at MoMA PS1. The 2026 edition of the exhibition brings together more than 50 artists from different backgrounds and age groups. The exhibition includes many types of art such as photography, installations, papier-mache, and tin foil sculptures. The show focuses on what it means to live in New York City in 2026 and highlights the everyday people who help keep the city running, including cab drivers, delivery bikers, and the people. The exhibition shows the diversity, creativity, and fast pace of life in New York through many different artistic styles and materials.

One of the ideas that many of the artists in Greater New York 2026 explore is the idea that everyone and everything is connected. This idea was shown clearly in María-Elena Pombo’s installation, which was made from reddish yarn hanging down from the ceiling. The artist created the yarn using avocado pits and water collected from different places around the world, including Nigeria, Japan, Venezuela, and also some areas in the United States. By using materials connected to many different countries, Pombo shows that New York City is part of a much larger global system. The hanging yarn also looked fragile and uneven, which made the installation feel delicate and emotional. I interpreted this as a reflection on migration and the way people from many different cultures come together in New York City. Even though the threads looked separate at times, they were all connected together as part of one installation. This could symbolize how people in New York come from different backgrounds but still help shape the city together. The work also reflects the fast-moving and constantly changing nature of city life. Overall, I thought this installation was very powerful and interesting because it used simple materials to express ideas about identity, migration, globalization, and human connection.

Another artwork that I found very cool was Dean Millien’s The Cats and the Rats (2026), which was made out of aluminum foil. At first, the piece looked playful and funny because of the different animal figures and the shiny material. However, the more I looked at it, the more I felt that the artwork had a deeper meaning. The cats appeared larger and more powerful, while the rats looked smaller and weaker. I interpreted this as representing power, survival, and the struggles that exist in everyday life, especially in a large city like New York. It also reminded me of Tom and Jerry, which made the piece feel more playful and familiar even though it still carried a deeper message underneath. The use of aluminum foil also made the sculptures feel fragile instead of strong and permanent. Even though foil is a simple household material, the artist transformed it into detailed animal figures, which made the work creative and interesting. The shiny silver surface gave the installation an artificial feeling, almost like the animals were part of a fantasy world instead of nature. I think the artist may be showing how life in the city can sometimes feel chaotic, competitive, and unpredictable. Overall, I liked this piece because it combined humor with deeper social ideas and turned ordinary materials into something expressive and memorable.

Overall, I enjoyed visiting the exhibition because each artwork explored modern life in a different way. Even though the pieces used very different materials and styles, many of them focused on themes such as identity, media culture, appearance, and life in New York City. The exhibition helped me better understand how contemporary artists use unusual materials and creative installations to express ideas about modern society and everyday experiences.
